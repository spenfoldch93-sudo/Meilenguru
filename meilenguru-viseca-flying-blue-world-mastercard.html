Where Viseca fits —
the honest version

Every other card in our Swiss miles card comparison earns Miles & More or a flexible points currency. The Viseca Flying Blue World Mastercard earns something different: Flying Blue miles, credited directly, with no transfer step in between. If Miles & More is your target programme, this card isn't for you — Cornèrcard or Swisscard will serve you better. If Flying Blue is your target, it's the most direct tool available in Switzerland.

We're going to compare it like-for-like against the cards we already recommend, including on the metric where it loses — welcome bonus. It's smaller. That's a real trade-off, not a footnote, and we'd rather say so plainly than bury it. But before the fee tables and comparison numbers, we want to say where we actually land on this, and why.

Where we land

The case for Flying Blue —
before the numbers

We think Flying Blue is the stronger programme for most Swiss Business Class collectors right now, and Viseca is the card we'd point a Flying Blue collector to first. That's a genuine opinion, formed from actually using both sides of this comparison — not just a way to explain away a smaller welcome bonus, though we know it can read that way. Which is exactly why the full, unflattering numbers are still coming right after this section, not buried at the bottom.

1 — In practice, it's just easier to find a seat

This is the argument that matters most to us, more than any single number on this page. In our own searching, Business Class availability has been noticeably easier to find on Flying Blue than on Miles & More, and Flying Blue's own booking tool — the calendar view, how clearly Promo Awards surface — is simply more usable than what SWISS and Lufthansa offer through Miles & More. That's a subjective call, not a statistic we can hand you, but it's the honest reason we think a smaller welcome bonus is worth accepting for the programme behind it.

2 — No fixed chart means no locked doors

Flying Blue has no fixed award chart. That cuts both ways — prices move with demand — but it also means there's no rigid "saver" allotment that quietly runs out months in advance the way some fixed-chart programmes do. Business Class Europe-North America typically runs from around 30,000-45,000 miles one-way during a monthly Promo Award, a 60,000-plus standard rate most of the time, and higher on peak dates — but the point is you can usually find something, at a price that reflects real demand, rather than staring at a chart showing zero saver seats for six months straight. This is the structural reason behind point 1 above.

3 — The seat itself, for what it's worth

One more opinion, held more loosely than the two above: we think Air France and KLM's current long-haul Business Class product is, on balance, a step ahead of what SWISS and Lufthansa are flying on comparable routes. That's genuinely closer to personal taste than the availability point — reasonable people who've flown both will disagree, and we're not claiming this is settled. We mention it because it's part of why we keep coming back to Flying Blue, not as a claim you should take at face value.

4 — The cheapest transfer ratio into Flying Blue from Switzerland

Revolut (Premium and above) transfers to Flying Blue at a full 1:1 ratio — better than the 2:1 Swiss Amex ratio, and better than what most Swiss residents assume is available. Metal (CHF ~19/month) earns 1 RevPoint per CHF 2 spent; Ultra earns 1 per CHF 1. Paired with Viseca as a second card, that's two undiluted paths into the same programme.

5 — You can buy your way to the finish line

Flying Blue sells miles directly via its Subscribe to Miles programme, from around €0.011/mile on the cheapest tier (3-month minimum commitment). No other Swiss miles programme on this site offers anything comparable — if you're 8,000 miles short of a Promo Award two weeks before you want to book, that gap is closeable with cash, not just patience.

Realistic Year 1 scenario — Flying Blue via Viseca + Revolut
  • Viseca welcome bonus (standard): 5,000 miles
  • Viseca spend (CHF 18k/yr @ 1 mile/CHF 2): 9,000 miles
  • Revolut Premium fill-in spend (CHF 750/mo @ 1 RevPoint/CHF 2, transferred 1:1): 4,500 miles
  • Total year 1: ~18,500 miles — smaller than the ~63,500-mile Miles & More scenario in our main comparison, but every mile is Flying Blue, with no conversion step, and it's enough on its own for a shorter-haul Promo Award redemption

A smaller welcome bonus isn't the same as a slower programme. In our experience, Flying Blue is simply the easier programme to actually book — and Viseca is the most direct way into it.

That's our honest lean, and we'd rather say so plainly than let it sit unstated between the lines. It doesn't erase the real numbers, though — Viseca does lose on welcome bonus, lounge access, and status earning, and we cover all three in full below, including where it loses outright. If your only goal is the largest possible number on day one, this isn't the card. If you're weighing which programme is actually worth building around, here's the card itself — including the parts that don't flatter it.

The card

Viseca Flying Blue World Mastercard —
in full

Issued by Viseca (the same company behind several of Switzerland's card programmes), this is a standard Mastercard with one distinguishing feature: every mile earned lands in your Flying Blue account directly, at the point of sale, with no separate transfer step and no conversion ratio to lose value to.

Why it works

  • Only Swiss-issued card crediting Flying Blue directly — no transfer ratio, no dilution
  • 1.5 miles per CHF 2 on Air France/KLM purchases specifically
  • Turnover bonuses: 4,000 miles at CHF 30k spend, 7,000 at CHF 60k, 9,000 at CHF 90k
  • 2,000-mile loyalty bonus on renewal, every year
  • Solid insurance bundle: CHF 20,000 trip cancellation, CHF 250,000 medical/repatriation, CHF 300,000 travel-accident, CHF 10,000 hire-car excess waiver, 2-year extended warranty
  • Miles don't expire as long as the card is used at least monthly

The trade-offs

  • 5,000-mile welcome bonus is well below Cornèrcard (10,000) or Amex (45,000)
  • Card spend earns miles only — no Flying Blue XP (elite-qualifying points)
  • No lounge access, no concierge — this is an earning tool, not a perks card
  • Requires an active Flying Blue membership to receive the bonus

One inconsistency worth flagging rather than hiding: Viseca's own general product page (viseca.ch/en/credit-cards/special-cards/flying-blue-world) doesn't list a welcome bonus at all — only the turnover and renewal bonuses above. The 5,000-mile figure comes from Viseca's dedicated Flying Blue landing page (card.viseca.ch/en/flyingblue/). We're using that page since it's the more specific, purpose-built source — but it's a reminder to verify the live figure yourself before applying, the same as with any other card on this site.

Miles & More, specifically → Cornèrcard or Swisscard

Viseca doesn't earn Miles & More at all. Don't buy this card for the wrong programme — see our full comparison instead.

If your goal is

The single biggest one-time miles event → Amex Platinum

45,000 MR points standard, transferable to Flying Blue at 2:1 (22,500 Flying Blue miles) or to Miles & More at the same ratio. Bigger than Viseca's welcome bonus even after the transfer loss — but you're paying CHF 900/year and diluting every point by half along the way.

If your goal is

Flying Blue, as your everyday driver → Viseca Flying Blue World

Every mile from daily spend lands directly, undiluted. Smaller welcome bonus, but no ratio tax on the spend that follows — and spend adds up faster than a one-time bonus does over a year of use.

If your goal is

Flying Blue, maximised across every channel → Viseca + Revolut + Amex

Run Viseca as the daily card, top up with Revolut Premium (1:1 transfer — the best ratio available), and let an Amex you already hold for other reasons feed in at 2:1 when its points would otherwise sit idle. None of these are exclusive.

FAQ

Frequently asked
questions

Is the Viseca Flying Blue World Mastercard worth it? +

Yes — and more strongly than the numbers alone suggest. It's the only Swiss-issued card that credits Flying Blue miles directly, with no transfer ratio and no dilution. The 5,000-mile welcome bonus is smaller than Cornèrcard or Amex, but in our own experience Flying Blue's Business Class availability has been easier to find and its booking tool more user-friendly than Miles & More's — which matters more to us than a bigger one-time bonus. Add the insurance bundle and the CHF 230 fee, and it's a reasonable everyday card for a Flying Blue collector specifically — less compelling only if Miles & More is your target programme.

Why do you recommend Flying Blue over Miles & More generally? +

Mainly experience, not a statistic we can point to: in our own searching, Business Class availability has been consistently easier to find on Flying Blue than on Miles & More, and Flying Blue's booking tool (calendar search, how clearly Promo Awards surface) is more user-friendly than what SWISS and Lufthansa offer. We'd also say, more as a matter of taste than fact, that we rate Air France and KLM's current Business Class product above SWISS and Lufthansa's on comparable routes. Reasonable people disagree on both points — this is our honest opinion after using both programmes, not a claim we can fully back with data.

How does Viseca compare to Cornèrcard and Swisscard? +

Cornèrcard and Swisscard both earn Miles & More, not Flying Blue, and both offer larger welcome bonuses (10,000 miles standard, sometimes elevated well beyond that). Viseca's welcome bonus is smaller, but it's the only one of the three that credits Flying Blue directly — there's no direct conversion path from Miles & More to Flying Blue at all.

Does the Viseca Flying Blue World Mastercard earn Flying Blue XP or elite status? +

No — card spend earns Flying Blue miles only, not XP (the points that count toward Flying Blue elite status). Status still has to come from flying or a separate route. Worth knowing upfront if elite status is part of your goal.

Can I combine the Viseca card with Amex or Revolut for Flying Blue? +

Yes. Viseca gives direct, undiluted earning on everyday spend. Revolut (Premium and above) transfers to Flying Blue at 1:1 and is a strong secondary path. Swiss Amex Membership Rewards transfer at 2:1 — less efficient, but still useful if you already hold Amex for its flexibility or welcome bonus. Running Viseca as your daily driver and topping up with Revolut or a Flying Blue miles subscription is a reasonable combination.
