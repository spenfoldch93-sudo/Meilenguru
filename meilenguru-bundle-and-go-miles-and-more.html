Bundle & Go —
is buying miles worth it?

A few thousand miles short of a redemption? Bundle & Go lets you top up your Miles & More balance directly. What it costs today, and a real worked example of when topping up beats paying cash outright.

What
Bundle & Go is

Bundle & Go is Miles & More's built-in miles-purchase feature, accessible directly from your account (transactions are processed by Points.com Inc., a Lufthansa Group partner). Instead of buying miles one at a time, you pick a fixed bundle — a set number of miles for a set price. The usual reason to use it: a redemption booking where you're a few thousand miles short, without booking another flight to make up the gap. As of this update, the three larger bundles also bundle in Avis car-rental perks, Priority Pass discounts, and a Lufthansa Business Lounge voucher — it's no longer purely a miles-for-cash exchange.

The basics

Prices
per bundle

Bundles come in four sizes, checked live in September 2026:

Bundle Miles Price Per mile
Small 5,000 miles €125 (~CHF 118) ~2.4 Rp.
Medium 50,000 miles €1,050 (~CHF 992) ~2.0 Rp.
Large 100,000 miles €1,850 (~CHF 1,748) ~1.7 Rp.
X-Large 150,000 miles €2,650 (~CHF 2,504) ~1.7 Rp.

Medium, Large and X-Large also come with a Lufthansa Business Lounge voucher (one-time access, valid 12 months) and Miles & More Points; all four include an Avis welcome-mile bonus and Priority Pass membership discounts. Billing runs in euros — at current exchange rates that's roughly the CHF amounts above, plus whatever foreign-transaction fee your card charges, which can range from 0-2.5% depending on the card.

No bonus is currently active
  • Miles & More has previously run limited-time bonuses on Bundle & Go — in the past, up to 50% extra miles on the base amounts
  • As of September 2026, no bonus is showing — the prices above are the bare rate
  • Always check your own account under Bundle & Go before buying: an active bonus changes the numbers below substantially, in your favour
The limit

The
annual cap

Miles & More caps miles purchased through Bundle & Go at 300,000 miles per calendar year, per account. Members can buy one bundle per transaction, but there's no limit on the number of transactions — you can combine bundles across the year up to the cap. For most people topping up a single redemption, this cap is essentially irrelevant.

The real numbers

A real
example

Rather than a rule of thumb, here's an actual redemption, checked live in September 2026: Zurich to Cape Town and back, Business Class, nonstop each way, dates in late October/early November. The miles and taxes below are what Miles & More's own booking engine showed for real, bookable seats.

Leg Miles Taxes & fees
Zurich → Cape Town 107,500 CHF 380
Cape Town → Zurich 76,507 ~CHF 285
Total round trip 184,007 miles ~CHF 665

The cheapest Business round-trip cash fare for the same route and week, booked directly on swiss.com: CHF 3,485. The question this article actually cares about: is it worth buying the miles to book the award seat instead of just paying that cash fare?

The answer depends entirely on how many of those 184,007 miles you'd need to buy versus how many you already have from card spend, transfers, or flying. Here's the same redemption priced at each shortfall:

If you're short... Bundle to buy All-in cost vs. CHF 3,485 cash
5,000 miles Small — CHF 118 CHF 783 −78%
50,000 miles Medium — CHF 992 CHF 1,657 −52%
100,000 miles Large — CHF 1,748 CHF 2,413 −31%
150,000 miles X-Large — CHF 2,504 CHF 3,169 −9%
All 184,007 miles, from zero X-Large + Medium — CHF 3,496 CHF 4,161 +19%

Topping up a shortfall of a few thousand or a few tens of thousands of miles is genuinely cheap relative to the cash fare it unlocks. Buying the entire redemption's miles from scratch is not — it comes out roughly a fifth more expensive than just paying cash, at today's bonus-free prices.

The math

When it's
worth it

Usually worth it when:
  • You're short a small-to-moderate number of miles for a specific redemption you've already found — not buying the whole thing from scratch
  • A bonus is active in your account — without one, the per-mile cost is meaningfully worse than the examples above
  • You compare the all-in cost (bundle price + the redemption's taxes) against the cash price of the same route, not just the bundle price in isolation

The old rule of thumb — "roughly 2 cents a mile, worth it for a business-class redemption" — assumed a 50% bonus that isn't running right now. Without it, Bundle & Go still earns its keep for closing a genuine shortfall, but stops making sense somewhere between the Large and X-Large tiers on a redemption this size. If you're buying most or all of a long-haul business redemption's miles from a standing start, run the cash price first — it may well be cheaper.

How much does Bundle & Go cost at Miles & More? +

Four bundles: €125 for 5,000 miles (Small), €1,050 for 50,000 miles (Medium), €1,850 for 100,000 miles (Large), and €2,650 for 150,000 miles (X-Large). Medium, Large and X-Large also include a Lufthansa Business Lounge voucher and Miles & More Points; all four include Avis and Priority Pass perks.

Is there a bonus on buying miles? +

Miles & More periodically runs bonus promotions on Bundle & Go. As of September 2026, no bonus is active — check the offer currently showing in your own account before buying, since an active bonus changes the value calculation significantly.

How many miles can I buy per year? +

The cap is 300,000 miles per calendar year, per account, via Bundle & Go.

What currency is Bundle & Go billed in? +

Euros, since Miles & More is processed through the Lufthansa Group (via Points.com). Swiss cards may add a foreign-transaction fee on top, depending on the card.

Is it worth buying all the miles for a business-class redemption from scratch? +

Usually not, at current bonus-free prices. A real Zurich-Cape Town business-class round trip checked live needed 184,007 miles; buying that many from scratch cost more than the cheapest cash business fare for the same route. Bundle & Go earns its keep when you're topping up a small shortfall on miles you already have, not when buying a redemption outright.
