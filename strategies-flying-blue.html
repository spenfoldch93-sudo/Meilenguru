Flying Blue —
the hidden gem

Criminally overlooked. Flying Blue has fewer earning options in Switzerland than Miles & More — but the monthly Promo Awards change everything. Discounts of 25-50% on award rates, every month, on a rotating set of routes. Most people don't know they exist. The ones who do book Business Class to New York for less than most people pay for Economy. It's also, in our own experience, simply the easier programme to actually book a seat with — more on that below.

Worth noting: card spend earns miles only, not Flying Blue XP (elite-qualifying points) — status still has to come from flying or a separate route.

Transfer · Amex Switzerland

Amex Membership Rewards → Flying Blue

Swiss Amex Membership Rewards points transfer to Flying Blue at a 2:1 ratio (2 MR points = 1 Flying Blue mile). The 45,000-point Amex Platinum welcome bonus becomes 22,500 Flying Blue miles. While the ratio is less favourable than global Amex cards, the welcome bonus is still the fastest single source of Flying Blue miles available to Swiss residents.

The Flying Blue Promo Awards advantage

Flying Blue has no fixed award chart — pricing floats with demand. Business Class Europe-North America typically runs from around 30,000-45,000 miles one-way during a Promo Reward, a 60,000-mile-plus standard rate most of the time, and well past 100,000 on peak dates. The number depends on the exact route, date, and demand — which is exactly why confirming live pricing on flyingblue.com before transferring or applying matters more here than with a fixed chart.

Monthly promotion

What are Promo Awards?

Flying Blue releases discounted award rates on a rotating set of routes every month — typically 25-50% below standard dynamic pricing. Published on the first of each month. Routes change — consistency and watchfulness is rewarded.

Key Flying Blue strategy notes
  • Never transfer speculatively: Flying Blue uses dynamic pricing — confirm award availability on flyingblue.com first, then transfer. Prices can move significantly day to day.
  • Don't wait for an Amex transfer bonus: unlike US or UK Amex cards, they're rare on Swiss Amex. Transfer once you've confirmed Promo Award availability — that's where the real saving is.
  • Promo Awards change monthly: Set a reminder for the 1st of each month. Routes from Geneva (GVA) and via Paris (CDG) or Amsterdam (AMS) regularly feature.
  • Miles expiry: Flying Blue miles expire after 24 months of inactivity. Any eligible activity (card spend, transfer, flight) resets the clock. Revolut transfers count as activity.
  • Book before 8 September 2026 for current terms: Flying Blue is splitting Air France- and KLM-operated award tickets into Light/Standard/Flex tiers. A Business award priced around 60,000 miles today (with lounge access and free changes included) becomes the new "Light" tier at the same 60,000 miles but without those perks — matching today's flexibility costs roughly 75,000 miles under "Standard" instead. Bookings confirmed before the 8th keep current terms. This only affects Air France/KLM-operated flights — partner-airline awards (Oman Air, and other Flying Blue partners) are unaffected.
  • Our honest take: in our own searching, Business Class availability has consistently been easier to find on Flying Blue than on Miles & More, and Flying Blue's booking tool (calendar search, how clearly Promo Awards surface) is more user-friendly than what SWISS and Lufthansa offer. We'd also say, more as a matter of taste than fact, that we rate Air France and KLM's current long-haul Business Class product a step ahead of SWISS and Lufthansa's on comparable routes — reasonable people who've flown both will disagree, and this isn't a claim we can fully back with data.
